# Read the charter's hard rows before writing a decision

- **What happened:** On 14 November the CEO said to use UI SDK. The lead decided to put it inside shipped components, pinned and wrapped, and cited principle 6 in support. A fresh reviewer failed the decision: principle 6 says nothing in brick may depend on pre-release software, and UI SDK is at 0.1.0-beta.3. The same review found twelve major problems, among them a list of new parts that only the steward may add, and an item marked done with no reviewer verdict. Evidence: `gates/raw/2026-11-14-ui-sdk-decision-review.json`; DECISIONS.md D-013 and A-16.
- **Cause:** The lead reasoned from the new facts and from memory of the charter, and did not open the charter. It checked the decision against the two budget rows it remembered (no JavaScript for core tasks, the size rows) and missed the rest. An instruction from the CEO also made the lead treat the question as "how", when part of it was "may we".
- **Change made:** Before the lead writes any decision that adds a dependency, a part, a token or a budget figure, it opens and searches three places for the thing being decided: the LOCK and budget rows in `charter/budget.md`, the rule and check lines of each principle in `charter/principles.md`, and the decision-rights table in `charter/operating-model.md`. The decision entry then quotes each row it touches. An instruction from the CEO that meets a charter rule is logged as a request for an exception, with the most reversible default, and is not treated as the exception.
- **Scope and reason:** Every entry in DECISIONS.md. The charter is long enough that memory of it is not reliable, and the lead wrote it only the day before.
- **Delete when:** A lint reads the dependency list and the part inventory and fails on a pre-release dependency or an unregistered part in brick, so that the check no longer rests on the lead's reading.
