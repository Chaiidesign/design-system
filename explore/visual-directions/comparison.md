# The three directions compared on both screens

Clay. Written by the lead on Fable on 14 November 2026, after a fresh reviewer measured all six pages (`gates/raw/2026-11-14-visual-directions-review.md`) and after the lead looked again at each page at 375 pixels in the light theme. It replaces a comparison the lead made on Sonnet on 13 November, which was in no file and which the review overturned on all three of its claims.

## What was measured

Every figure here is the reviewer's, from the rendered pages at 320 and 375 pixels in light and dark.

- **All three pass every hard row** in all 24 page states: text contrast, no sideways scroll at 320 pixels, field text of 16 pixels or more, controls of 48 pixels or more, a visible focus ring, and errors not shown by colour alone.
- **The differences are in what stands out.**

| | Ledger | Instrument | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size of the total on checkout | 20 px, the same as section headings; the page title is 36 px | 36 px, the largest type on the page | 54 to 56 px, in a full-width block |
| Total visible on the first screen at 320 px | No | Yes, the only one | No |
| "Drafted by Souvenir" warning | Ink text with an amber rule and icon | Grey text and a grey pencil; the quietest thing on its screen | Framed, with a yellow block; the loudest |
| The person's own message | Indented serif italic, no fill | A bubble 1.23 to 1 against the ground in light, 1.09 to 1 in dark | A black block, the loudest thing on its screen |
| Height of the assistant page at 320 px | 1478 px | 1468 px | 2184 px, and the proposal does not fit one screen |
| Defects found | Total not prominent; "Delivery" breaks mid-word; a wrapped button has no padding | The warning is too quiet; panels flatten together in dark | An email address breaks mid-word; the focus ring touches the next button; length |

## What the lead got wrong on 13 November

- *"The real choice is between Ledger and Instrument."* Not supported. Signal is the only direction with no failure against the stated criteria on either screen.
- *"Signal is the weakest for a conversation."* True only of its length. It separates the person, the proposal and the warning most strongly. Instrument is the weakest at that.
- *"Ledger's warning is the quietest of the three."* False. Instrument's is.
- In DECISIONS.md A-13 the lead also wrote that Ledger's cost summary "is the clearest of the three". The measurements say the opposite: it is the least prominent.

## The lead's judgment now

This section is judgment, not measurement.

- **Ledger stays the working direction, for a narrower reason than before.** It still needs the fewest parts: one surface, rules, no tonal layers. Its defects are the cheapest kind: one type size, one wrap, one padding value. It is no longer preferred for its cost summary.
- **Three corrections come with it,** because a direction that hides the total fails the first slice: the total becomes the largest type on the checkout page; the term column of the cost summary may not break a word; a button keeps its padding when its label wraps.
- **Instrument is the close alternative.** Its checkout is the clearest of the three and it looks most like what people already know from a phone. What holds the lead back is structural: its hierarchy rests on tonal layers, and in dark those layers sink to within 1.09 to 1.38 of each other. Fixing that means more surface tokens, where Ledger needs none.
- **Signal is not ruled out by any measurement.** The lead's case against it is judgment: on the assistant screen the loudest thing is the person's own message, when the thing that needs checking is the proposal; and everything being bold leaves no quiet register for secondary content.
- **What would change the lead's mind:** the CEO valuing a familiar chat feel over the fewest parts (then Instrument, with its warning and dark theme fixed); or a test with people showing that Ledger's italic is not read as "my message".

No direction shows a visible "You" or "Souvenir" label. In all three, who is speaking is carried by styling alone. That is a defect of the shared content, to fix whichever direction is chosen.
